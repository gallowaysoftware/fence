# Shark
Path: nothing but nerve. Scavenge every night, flip whatever comes in; hard bargains with sellers who need cash, markups on buyers in a hurry; threats when the odds favour you. Rock Bottom is the native start.
Buy: Landlord Grace (100) the first day it is affordable; Seller Referral (200) by day 10; Showcase I; then whatever engine your best find points to.
Pivot: the first machine you find or win is your engine; adopt its path's order.
Factions: hard on Upper Levels and tourists; fair to Black Market (losses double) and Lower Levels.
Never: be generous for its own sake; let a faction come within 30 of -100; threaten Security.
Windfall: everything is a windfall; flip it unless it starts an engine.
Win: survive the first month, then the buyout by any means.

## Payback nights (hard; cost ÷ net per night, derived — the shark has no engine, only flow)

| Item | Cost | Effect | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landlord Grace (150) | 150 | one missed-rent forgiveness — the run-saver | insurance, day 1 |
| Seller Referral (300 + 10/n) | 300 | sellers referred to the store: more stock to flip | traffic, unmeasured |
| Showcase I (300) | 300 | +1 case visitor type | traffic |
| first machine found/won | 0 | adopts its path's whole order | the pivot |

**Reading:** the shark owns nothing, so every number is marginal flow: scavenge (48 trips in
the reference run) + counter margin (15 %) + hard bargains. The only capital worth holding is
Grace (day 1) — a missed rent ends the run, and the shark runs the thinnest cash floor of any
archetype. Referrals and Showcase buy *traffic*, which is the shark's only input; the 10/night
fees are the leak to watch (73/week for the pair). The pivot rule is the real strategy: the
first machine found or won converts the run to that path's payback order — the shark is the
entry state of every other archetype, which is why Rock Bottom is its native start.
